## The short version

Openly shows you other users who are nearby and currently "open" to meeting. To do that it needs
your approximate location while you are open, and a name and emoji you choose. You never give us
your real name, phone number, or email. There are no ads and no analytics, and we do not sell or
share your data with anyone.

## What we collect

**Account.** When you first open the app it creates an anonymous account. There is no email,
password, or phone number — just a random identifier. We cannot tell who you are from it.

**Profile you choose.** A display name, an emoji, and the interests you select. Use a nickname if
you prefer; nothing checks that the name is real.

**Location — only while you are "open".** When you switch yourself to open, the app sends your
current coordinates so that nearby users can see you as a blip on their radar. When you switch off,
location updates stop. Other users are shown your approximate distance and bearing, not your
address or your exact coordinates.

**Messages and attachments.** Once two people both express interest, a chat opens. Text messages,
photos you attach, and voice notes you record are stored so they can be delivered. Alongside each
message we store when it was sent, when it was read, and any emoji reactions. While you are typing,
a short-lived flag is stored so the other person sees the typing indicator; it expires within
seconds.

**Notification token.** A push token issued by the operating system, used solely to notify you of
new messages.

**Permissions the app requests.** Location (to appear on the radar), notifications (new messages),
microphone (voice notes only, while you hold the record button), and photo selection (only the
specific image you pick — the app never browses your library).

## What we do not collect

No contacts, no call or SMS data, no browsing history, no advertising identifier, no device
fingerprint, no background location, and no analytics or crash-reporting SDK. There are no
third-party trackers in the app.

## How your data is used

Only to make the app work: to place you on other users' radar, to deliver your messages, and to
notify you when someone messages you. We do not use it for advertising, profiling, or training
machine-learning models, and we do not sell it.

## Who can see what

**Other users nearby** see your display name, emoji, interests, and approximate distance — but only
while you are open. **People you have matched with** see your messages and attachments.
**Google Firebase** stores the data on our behalf as our infrastructure provider (see Google's
privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy). Nobody else.

## How long we keep it

Your profile and messages are kept until you delete them or delete your account. Location is
overwritten with each update and is not kept as a history — we store where you are now, not where
you have been. Typing indicators expire in seconds.

## Your choices

- **Stop sharing location:** switch yourself to not-open, or revoke the location permission in
  Android settings. The app works for reading existing chats without it.
- **Delete your account and data:** [DESCRIBE THE IN-APP PATH, e.g. "Settings → Delete account"].
  This removes your profile, your matches, and your messages.
- **Turn off notifications:** revoke the notification permission in Android settings.

> **Play requirement:** Google requires apps with accounts to offer account deletion, including a
> web-accessible route. If the in-app path above does not exist yet, build it before submitting —
> this is a common rejection reason.

## Security

Traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit (TLS). Access to stored data is
restricted by server-side security rules so that you can only read your own profile and the chats
you are part of. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
